# Abstract.
abstract = "Why are (some) bodily sounds more disgusting than others? Sensory cues of pathogen threat can elicit disgust, facilitating avoidance. Research has focused primarily on the visual processing of cues to this threat, despite evidence that auditory processing is prioritized as well. Here, we investigated whether disgust tracks auditory cues associated with pathogen transmission. Because expulsion facilitates pathogen transmission, we predicted greater disgust toward expulsion than non-expulsion sounds. Moreover, because bodily fluids allow viable pathogens to persist longer in the environment than gases, and fluids house pathogen types likely present in ancestral populations, we predicted greater disgust toward fluid than gaseous expulsion. Across 17,333 participants from 58 countries, preregistered analyses supported these predictions, with minimal cross-national variation. Follow-up analyses revealed that sounds perceived as wetter and more associated with fluid expulsion elicited greater disgust. Disgust toward pathogen-relevant auditory cues may thus be part of a species-typical disease-avoidance psychology."
